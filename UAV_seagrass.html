Research

Coastal Seagrass Mapping

Drones, field ecology, and AI for monitoring eelgrass health and disease from Alaska to Southern California.

Funded by the National Science Foundation (NSF Build and Broaden, B2)

Seagrass meadows stabilize shorelines, filter water, shelter marine life, and store blue carbon. GeoFly Lab pairs low-altitude drone mapping with field ecology and AI-driven geospatial analysis to monitor these habitats with site-level detail and coast-wide coverage.

Coverage
About 18° of latitude, Alaska to Southern California
Survey sites
33 sites in 7 regions, surveyed since 2019
Data
More than 50,000 drone images and videos
Funding

Background

Why seagrass matters

Seagrass meadows are an important part of the global carbon cycle. Through photosynthesis they take up carbon dioxide and store it in leaves, roots, and underlying sediments, acting as long-term carbon sinks. They also provide habitat for marine life, protect coastlines, and cycle nutrients.

Along the U.S. West Coast, eelgrass (Zostera marina) is the dominant species, often making up 50 to 90 percent of seagrass cover. It faces growing pressure from wasting disease, associated with Labyrinthula zosterae, which causes dark lesions, loss of green tissue, and in severe cases plant death. Warming water, nutrient loading, and sedimentation can raise disease risk, so rapid, spatially explicit monitoring is essential for conservation and restoration.

Study area

Where we work

Since 2019, our team and collaborators have built a regional dataset spanning about 18° of latitude along the Pacific coast. We survey during summer low tides and pair drone imagery with on-the-ground measurements of eelgrass health and disease.

Methods

How we do it

We combine RGB and multispectral drone imagery with AI and computer vision to turn pixels into ecological insight.

Green Leaf Area Index

G-LAI maps quantify photosynthetically active (green) eelgrass tissue across whole meadows.

Lesion analysis

EeLISA analysis of georeferenced eelgrass blades measures wasting-disease severity and validates remote sensing products.

AI segmentation

Segmentation workflows based on the Segment Anything Model separate eelgrass from algae and sand, measure loss of greenness, and track disease over time.
