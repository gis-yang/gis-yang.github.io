GeoFly Lab members **Griffin Svec-Burdick** and **Nathan Leroy** have contributed georeferenced aerial photographs to the new publication, "Histories of Fire and Industry in the Santa Cruz Mountains." This work, in collaboration with Professor **Andrew Mathews**, delves into the intertwined narratives of people, plants, and fire that shape the region's landscape.

The Santa Cruz Mountains have a rich history deeply connected to fire, as reflected in the creation stories of the Amah Mutsun Tribal band and other Ohlone people. These narratives often feature the hummingbird, or 'Umunhum' in the Mutsun language, symbolizing the vital role of fire.

Today, alongside the enduring legacies of indigenous lifeways disrupted by colonialism, contemporary challenges like climate change are increasingly influencing these landscapes. Histories of settler logging, ranching, and housing developments, combined with changing environmental conditions, are contributing to heightened flammability in Central California's coastal environments.
